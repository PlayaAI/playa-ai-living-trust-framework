# Living Trust Framework Overview

**The Playa AI Living Trust Framework is an open community AI stewardship resource that helps artists, builders and cultural organizations decide—on their own terms—how AI enters their creative and cultural spaces.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> “Living Trust” is a project name and governance metaphor. It does not create an estate-planning trust, fiduciary relationship, certification or legal entity.

## Stewardship model

The Framework is a layered resource rather than a single policy:

1. The [Charter](CHARTER.md) states constitutional commitments.
2. Stewardship domains organize the questions communities and adopters must address.
3. Profiles contain testable controls for defined risks and contexts.
4. Protocols translate the Framework into field practice.
5. Templates and playbooks help adopters document and communicate decisions.
6. Governance records evidence, exceptions, dissent and revision.

## Six domains

### 1. Community authority and participation

Who is affected? Who decides? Who is missing from the room? This domain covers stakeholder identification, compensated participation, decision rights, dissent, appeals and the ability to revisit a deployment.

### 2. Consent, privacy and data stewardship

What is collected, inferred, shared, retained or used for AI? This domain covers understandable consent, purpose limitation, minimization, security, rights workflows, deletion and honest technical limitations. The current [Data Stewardship Profile](standard/standard.md) contains the Framework's first auditable controls.

### 3. Creative agency, authorship and attribution

How does AI affect creative labor, credit, provenance, style, likeness, compensation and control over future use? Requirements in this domain remain a co-design agenda pending artist and legal review.

### 4. Cultural memory and relational data

Who has authority over archives, stories, traditions and artifacts that represent more than one person? This domain considers collective harm, cultural protocols, contextual integrity, posthumous interests and the limits of individual permission.

### 5. AI disclosure, human override and contestability

When and how is AI present? Can people choose a meaningful alternative, challenge an output, reach an accountable person, or pause the system? [EELP](protocols/eelp.md) is the Framework's field implementation protocol for AI-mediated experiences.

### 6. Accountability, learning and public benefit

What evidence demonstrates responsible operation? How are incidents, exceptions, funding, conflicts, failures and community feedback recorded? What learning can safely return to the commons?

## Adoption lifecycle

1. **Convene:** identify affected people, authority and cultural context.
2. **Define:** document the purpose, AI role, non-AI path and prohibited uses.
3. **Assess:** apply relevant profiles and protocols; record gaps and exceptions.
4. **Test:** run accessible, consent-based pilots with stop criteria.
5. **Decide:** publish responsible owners, evidence, dissent and unresolved risks.
6. **Operate:** monitor impacts, incidents, requests and vendor changes.
7. **Return:** share reusable learning without exposing or appropriating participants.
8. **Revisit:** renew, change or end the use through a scheduled community process.

## Current maturity

Only the Data Stewardship Profile contains normative controls. Other cultural-stewardship domains are proposed and must not be described as ratified, certified or community-validated. The [co-design agenda](co-design/AGENDA.md) defines the work required before those domains become normative.
