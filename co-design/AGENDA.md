# Cultural AI Stewardship Co-Design Agenda

## Status

This agenda identifies questions for artist, builder, cultural-organization and affected-community engagement. It is not a set of normative requirements and must not be described as community-validated.

## 1. Community authority and participation

- Which people and communities are materially affected by the proposed AI use?
- Who holds formal, cultural and lived authority to participate in the decision?
- How will participation be accessible, compensated and protected from retaliation?
- What decisions can the community change, pause or reject?
- How will dissent and unresolved disagreement be preserved?

## 2. Creative agency, authorship and attribution

- When does AI assist, transform, imitate or substitute for human creative work?
- What credit, provenance and disclosure do artists and audiences expect?
- Which uses of style, likeness, voice, archives or prior work require permission?
- How should compensation and bargaining power be addressed?
- What non-AI path should remain available to creators and participants?

## 3. Cultural memory and relational data

- Who is represented in a story, archive, recording or artifact beyond the contributor?
- Which community laws, protocols, custodians or restrictions apply?
- What should never be placed into a model, public dataset or generated experience?
- How should withdrawal, posthumous interests and collective harm be handled?
- What learning can safely return to the commons, and who decides?

## 4. AI disclosure, human override and contestability

- What must people know before encountering or participating with the system?
- Can they decline without exclusion from the underlying cultural experience?
- Who can explain, correct, override or suspend the system?
- What failure, complaint or harm triggers escalation?
- How will the organization identify people who may need additional safeguards?

## 5. Accountability and public benefit

- What evidence would demonstrate that the use benefits the community it names?
- Who owns continuing monitoring and incident response?
- How are funder, vendor, artist and institutional conflicts disclosed?
- When will the community reconsider whether the AI use should continue?
- How will adaptations and failures be published without exposing participants?

## Promotion path

A proposed cultural-stewardship requirement may become normative only after:

1. documented engagement with affected artists and communities;
2. accessibility, equity, legal, privacy, security and implementation review;
3. pilot evidence from more than one organizational context;
4. public comment with published disposition of material objections; and
5. approval under the Framework's governance process.
